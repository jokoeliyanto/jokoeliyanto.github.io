Sentiment analysis is a technique used to identify opinions, comments, feedback, or attitudes expressed in textual content and classify them into positive, negative, or neutral categories. In the context of elections, sentiment analysis is widely employed to predict election outcomes by examining public opinions toward candidates. Positive public sentiment is generally considered an indicator of voter support and may serve as a reference for forecasting election results. To improve sentiment classification, fuzzy logic can be incorporated by assigning each opinion a degree of membership to positive, negative, and neutral sentiment classes. This study compares two fuzzy logic–based approaches, namely Fuzzy Naïve Bayes and Fuzzy Sentiment using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). The methods were evaluated using public opinions collected from Twitter during the Solo and Medan regional elections (PILKADA) between September and December 2020. The performance of both approaches was compared after being applied to the collected dataset. The findings indicate that different fuzzy-based approaches produce different classification results, highlighting the influence of the selected method on sentiment analysis performance.
